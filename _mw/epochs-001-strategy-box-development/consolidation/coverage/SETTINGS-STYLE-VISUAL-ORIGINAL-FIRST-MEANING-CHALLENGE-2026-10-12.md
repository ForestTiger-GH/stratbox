### Explicit tension: two original style fallback proposals

The Settings study's visible standard fallback (`0044 L2324–L2350`) and the later atomic StyleSet study's unresolved-by-default behavior (`0042 L1068–L1102`) cannot be flattened into one unconditional fallback promise. Both are dated **Research proposals**, sharing the same future product context rather than two independent deployed observations. Both reject *silent* provider substitution. Their substantive difference is **whether a new Run may proceed before an explicit authorized fallback rule exists**. Maintained Science now preserves the difference, narrows any permissive fallback to **separately authorized** conditions, and states that a strict identity/brand requirement can block output. It remains a Decision for Product/managed policy owner, not a Scientific or provider choice.

### Explicit tension: optional density and OS reduced-motion

Visual Research proposed two desktop-density modes and a portable preferences shape (`0043 L1864–L1907, L1980–L1994`). Settings Research explicitly postpones exposing density until test evidence establishes value, and prefers following the operating system's reduced-motion signal. They are competing UX proposals informed by the same historical Windows environment, not two independent acceptance studies. The user may eventually choose Compact without making it a mandatory first-release Setting. Conversely, a reliable reduced-motion **rendering behavior** can be an accessibility obligation even without a visible extra toggle. Current Science keeps that distinction.

## 2. Exact code reality compared with source recommendations

At `stratbox-windows@959e9c4ce1441124af5111c1e025041714e04d3b`, `runtime/config.py` has a mutable mixed `AppUserConfig` and direct `Path.write_text` writer. Its behavior was characterized by [Actions 38207727483](https://github.com/ForestTiger-GH/stratbox/actions/runs/38207727483), `stratbox@5ba2176845f4f0881ea024b1d7283069a601ac04`: **eight original config-function cases passed**, followed by **two PreferencesService mutation-before-save cases** at [CI 38207892194](https://github.com/ForestTiger-GH/stratbox/actions/runs/38207892194) (`stratbox@e37185b22577b96927e0b3bc896b5754e17d4e46`, **SUCCESS**), comprising missing-file write-on-load, malformed JSON typed error, integer-coercion ValueError, nonempty `"false"` becoming True, nonpositive geometry acceptance, verbatim artificial form-value serialization, injected partial-write loss, and unversioned mixed schema. The malformed-value and synthetic sensitive-field cases overlap existing earlier WEX-08/WEX-09; these are **not new independent corroboration**. The two new PreferencesService cases establish that the service mutates the in-memory preference or Scenario draft before calling the actual save writer, so a write rejection can leave a new visible/in-process value and unchanged disk bytes. No real user settings were overwritten and no native Windows power-loss or installed host was tested.

The scientific inference is limited but material: current in-place config save does **not** provide the proposed temp+replace recovery guarantee. A UI appearance update might also succeed on screen without being durable. The current implementation behavior belongs in `docs/current-how/windows-application.md`; the separate failure interpretation in Science §7 does not claim to be a patch or a promise of future backend atomicity.
